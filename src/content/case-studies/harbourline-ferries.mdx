## Overview

Harbourline runs six ferry routes between the mainland and three islands. When weather or a mechanical fault cancels a sailing, every passenger on it needs a new one, and almost all of them used to phone.

I joined as Lead Product Designer for twelve weeks to find out why self-service was failing, and to fix it.

## The problem

On a disrupted day, the phone queue reached <Metric note="average wait across the ten worst disruption days of the previous winter, from the contact centre's own reporting">47 minutes</Metric>. Passengers were stuck on a harbour wall, on hold, while the app showed them a generic cancellation banner and nothing else.

### What I was asked to do

- Understand why passengers phoned instead of using the app
- Design a way to rebook a disrupted journey without calling
- Give crew on the quayside something useful to say

## Research

I spent two disruption days on the quayside with crew, and ran eleven interviews with passengers who had been caught out that winter.

![A journey map of a disrupted crossing, from the cancellation notice to boarding a later sailing](/case-studies/harbourline-ferries/journey-map.svg)

Three things came up again and again:

1. The cancellation message said what had happened, but not what to do next
2. Rebooking in the app meant cancelling first, and nobody trusted that they would get a seat back
3. Crew had no more information than passengers did

| Finding | Passengers affected | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No next step in the message | Almost all | Interviews, call recordings |
| Fear of losing the booking | Most | Interviews |
| Crew as uninformed as passengers | Everyone on the quay | Observation |

## Design

<DecisionLog
  title="Rebook in place, never cancel first"
  chose="A single 'Move to another sailing' action that holds the original booking until the new one is confirmed."
  rejected="Keeping the existing cancel-then-rebook flow and explaining it better."
  because="Research showed the fear of losing a seat, not the number of steps, was what sent people to the phone. No amount of explanation fixes a flow people are right to distrust."

The rebooking flow puts the next three available sailings first, with the one leaving soonest highlighted.

## Outcome

In the pilot on two routes, rebooking a disrupted journey took <Metric note="median time from opening the disruption notice to a confirmed new sailing, across the pilot routes">50 seconds</Metric> in the app, against 14 minutes on the phone.

## What I would do next

Extend the disruption message to crew handsets, so passengers and crew see the same options at the same moment.
